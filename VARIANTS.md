# Variants — how the entry was picked

Seven one-line patch notes were drafted and scored against the judge's
published criteria, most important first:

1. A single-line patch note for a bug in real life / human biology.
2. Genuinely funny on a first read; the joke rests on a real comedic
   idea, not just the word "bug" dropped into a sentence.
3. Reads like an actual release note: version number, terse engineering
   register, fix stated as if it shipped.
4. Names a specific, recognisable bug of the body or daily life, so the
   reader winces and laughs at once.
5. Uses its 160 characters well: no padding, no setup the punchline
   does not need.

## The shortlist

| # | Text | Chars | Verdict |
|---|------|-------|---------|
| 1 | v2.3.1: fixed a bug where entering a room wiped the quest objective from memory until the player left again. | 108 | **CHOSEN** |
| 2 | v3.7.0: fixed a bug where the brain buffered the perfect comeback 4 hours after the argument ended. | 99 | Runner-up |
| 3 | v4.2.0: fixed a bug where the mouth shipped sentences before the brain finished loading them. | 93 | Strong |
| 4 | v1.7.3: fixed a bug where the little toe clipped through furniture and dealt full damage to the player. | 103 | Strong |
| 5 | v2.6.0: fixed a bug where a song heard once looped in the brain for 72 hours with no mute button. | 97 | Good |
| 6 | v3.4.1: fixed a bug where the stomach growled during silent meetings, ignoring the Do Not Disturb setting. | 106 | Good |
| 7 | v2.8.2: fixed a bug where the brain saved a dream to the wrong folder and replayed it as a memory. | 98 | Good |

## Why #1 won

- **Most universal + most specific at once.** The doorway effect
  (walk in, forget why, remember on the way out) happens to everyone,
  and the note names the exact weird mechanism — memory returns only
  when you leave — instead of gesturing at a generic annoyance.
- **The punchline is the fix.** "Wiped the quest objective from
  memory" is a real comedic idea in changelog register, not a joke
  with "bug" sprinkled on it.
- **Wince + laugh.** Everyone recognises the moment of standing
  blank in a doorway.
- **Clean of the field's clichés.** The public submissions already
  lean hard on the 3am brain replay, the bladder, hiccups, and the
  brief's own left-ear example. #1 shares no premise with any of
  those, so it does not read as a copy of a stronger-known line.

## Why the others lost

- **#2 (late comeback):** excellent and nearly tied — "buffered" is a
  perfect engineering verb for the feeling. It loses only because the
  doorway effect is recognised a beat faster, and #1's "until the
  player left again" twist is the sharper second half.
- **#3 (mouth before brain):** funny, but "foot in mouth" is a
  familiar idiom; the joke is a rephrase of a saying rather than a
  fresh observation.
- **#4 (little toe):** vivid and wincing, but "stepping on furniture
  hurts" is a premise half the field will also reach for, and it has
  no twist beyond the pain itself.
- **#5 (earworm):** relatable, but "no mute button" is the expected
  punchline; the reader sees it coming.
- **#6 (stomach in meetings):** good and specific, but the joke is
  embarrassment, not a bug with a mechanism.
- **#7 (dream saved as memory):** clever, but it reads more like a
  shower thought than a shipped fix, and it asks the reader to think
  twice — the winner lands on the first read.

All seven are under 160 characters and single-line; the program ships #1.
